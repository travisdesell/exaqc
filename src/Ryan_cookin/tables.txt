================================================================================
Stage summaries (one paragraph each)
================================================================================

Stage A -- baseline. Trained encoder parameters, hand-built ansatz (per-qubit
RY + linear CNOT chain, depth 2, identical across cells). N is swept manually.
4 encoder classes (fixed_angle, fixed_amplitude, fixed_basis, learned) x 4 N x
4 datasets = 64 cells. Cheapest to run; deterministic; useful as the
"encoder-choice-only" axis. Establishes that learned > others by a lot, and
that the best N is dataset-dependent.

Stage B -- evolved ansatz, manual N. Replace Stage A's hand-built ansatz with
a CircuitGenome searched by EXAQC. Same encoders, same N sweep. Isolates
"does evolving the ansatz help?" relative to A. Where every dataset champion
lives in our single-seed runs. After Stage D added two more encoders, Stage B
also ran on linear_proj and reupload_euler (now 6 x 4 x 4 = 96 cells).

Stage C -- evolved ansatz + evolved N. Stage B + register-evolution mutations
(grow_register, shrink_register) so EXAQC decides the qubit count too. Drops
the manual N axis: 6 encoders x 4 datasets x seed = 24 cells. Best mean
test_acc across all stages on single-seed (0.862). Rescues fixed_amplitude
(it wants N = log2(D), which only Stage C finds automatically).

Stage D -- universal-encoder additions. Not a driver. Adds two new encoder
classes to encoders.py:
  - linear_proj  : RY(W*x + b) with full N x D learnable matrix; depth=2
  - reupload_euler: per-qubit Rot(alpha, beta, gamma) where each angle is its
                    own learnable linear function of features; depth=2
Both feed Stage B and Stage C via the --encoders flag. Their addition was
the single biggest accuracy improvement we've shipped -- reupload_euler at
~0.98 mean is well above the previous best (learned at ~0.90).

Stage E -- full co-evolution, weak encoder primitives. A single CircuitGenome
contains both encoder gates (enc_ry/rx/rz, each single-axis, single-feature,
2 params) and ansatz gates. Encoder structure + ansatz structure + N all
evolved. v1: 120 genomes / pop 25. v2: 300 genomes / pop 30 to test "is it
budget-limited?" Result: v2 changed mean by -0.003 -- the bottleneck was the
gate vocabulary, not the search budget.

Stage F -- universal-gate-set co-evolution. Redesign of Stage E using
universal primitives so evolution can place ONE gate to get full SU(2) or
universal-2q capacity, instead of having to compose them.
  v1 (hybrid)        : enc_rot (universal-1q feature-dep, 6 params) +
                       scalar-trained rxx/ryy/rzz + ry/rx/rz/cx/cz
  v2 (full feat-dep) : enc_rot + enc_xx/yy/zz (feature-dep 2q Isings, 3 params
                       each, together span the canonical 2q gate / KAK core)
                       + ry/rx/rz/cx/cz
v2 leaps over Stage E by +0.114 mean test_acc; v1 is only +0.014. The lever
that mattered was making 2-qubit gates feature-dependent, not making
1-qubit gates universal. Multi-seed and 300-genome follow-ups confirm.

================================================================================
Champion circuit composition (qubits, depth, 1q / 2q gate counts)
================================================================================

For each (stage, dataset) champion we rebuild the same qnode the stage
driver ran in production -- external encoder (where applicable) plus the
evolved genome -- then walk the tape and report:

  N        : full register size (genome's input qubits, including any wires
             that never get touched -- so this is the search-space size, not
             the wires-actually-used count).
  depth    : greedy parallel depth -- ops on disjoint wires share a layer.
  n_1q     : 1-qubit ops in the executed tape.
  n_2q     : 2-qubit ops (cx, cz, rxx/yy/zz, IsingXX/YY/ZZ, ...).
  n_multi  : ops with >=3 wires (chiefly AmplitudeEmbedding when the
             fixed_amplitude encoder is in play -- it stays as one
             multi-qubit op rather than decomposing).
  test_acc : same number reported in the headline tables above.

Things this composition table makes visible that the headline numbers
don't:

* Stage A circuits are the heaviest by far (n_1q + n_2q = 52-60). Every
  cell is the same shape: external encoder + 2-layer RY/CNOT ansatz.
* Stage B keeps the encoder but the ansatz shrinks (evolved CircuitGenome
  added 6-19 ops). Champion B cells use 7-21 n_1q and 6-19 n_2q.
* Stage C shrinks further because evolved-N often picks small registers
  (N=4 on 3/4 datasets) -- depth drops to 7-12.
* Stage E v1 circuits are EXTREMELY 1q-heavy and 2q-light (e.g. breast_cancer
  N=6 with 7 single-qubit gates and 0 two-qubit gates -- a literally
  classical single-wire classifier dressed in a 6-qubit shell).
* Stage F v2 champions look balanced (n_1q ~ n_2q), matching the design
  intent that universal 2-qubit gates show up alongside the universal
  1-qubit encoder gates.
* Multi-seed cells are NOT a strict subset structurally -- different
  seeds find different gate counts even when test_acc matches. e.g.
  F_v2_multiseed iris champion is N=6, 5 1q + 5 2q gates, where the seed=0
  F_v2 iris champion was N=7, 7 1q + 7 2q. Same accuracy ceiling (1.000
  vs 0.967), very different circuits.
* n_multi > 0 only happens when fixed_amplitude is the champion -- the
  AmplitudeEmbedding op stays as one multi-wire op.

================================================================================
How to read the tables
================================================================================

* Staged designs (B, C) win the headline. Winners-only multi-seed mean is
  ~0.95 and best is 1.000 on 3/4 datasets. reupload_euler is co-champion
  essentially everywhere.

* Co-evolution stages cluster below staged. E v1/v2 and F v1 all sit at
  0.79-0.80 mean. Stage F v2 jumps to 0.89-0.90 by making 2-qubit gates
  feature-dependent. Still ~0.06 below the staged ceiling.

* Two findings from the follow-ups:
  (a) Stage F v2's iris jump is robust at the ceiling (3/3 seeds at
      0.989 +- 0.019).
  (b) Stage F v2 IS search-limited on big datasets -- breast_cancer
      gained +0.035 from 2.5x budget. Stage E v2 (same budget bump on
      the weaker gate vocabulary) gained nothing. The architecture
      really was the bottleneck.

* Single-seed champions can mislead. The iris seed=0 Stage B/learned 1.000
  is a lucky draw; true 3-seed mean is 0.989 +- 0.019. Always look at
  multi-seed before trusting a perfect-score cell.


================================================================================
Future / planned stages
================================================================================

Stage F v3 (open) -- KAK macro-gate. Add enc_can or enc_kak as a single
3-param (canonical-core only) or 15-param (full SU(4)) primitive. Tests
"evolve placement of universal 2-qubit unitaries" vs "evolve the internal
structure of one." Stage F v2 has the building blocks but no template;
v3 would supply the template as a single primitive.

Noise studies (open) -- All current sweeps are noise-free. Stage A's
NaN-fix work enabled noise; worth re-running the 3 winning encoders
(learned, linear_proj, reupload_euler) under an IBM noise model to see
whether the staged ranking holds when decoherence is in the picture.
If the universal-encoder dominance is less robust under noise, evolved
structure (Stage F) might catch up or win.

Multi-seed Stage A (open) -- The full 64-cell Stage A rerun across 3
seeds was estimated at ~17 hr. Not done. Stage A still single-seed
in all tables above.

Parameter-efficiency frontier (open) -- Champions all use tiny gate
counts (1-5 enabled) but encoders have very different param costs
(learned: 4N vs reupload_euler: 6*N*(D+1)). Worth a Pareto plot for the
paper writeup.
